the founder's commandments (for trove)

  • It's yours, not an audience's. No likes, no followers, no metrics theater. "Me time."
  • Success = you feel replenished and go make something — not time-on-screen. If it ever optimizes for scroll, it's the wrong thing.
  • The bar for every image is taste. Ugly things "detract from the experience." It should feel hand-picked.
  • The grid is possibility, not a list. Wandering, window-shopping, museum-calm. Image is the interface; strip the chrome.
  • Algorithms must "feel human" — a friend with good taste, never a slot machine.
  • Wellbeing is engineered on purpose. Calm, quiet, dark, generous space.
  • Watch one real person use it ("what do you expect to happen? …is that what you saw?"), fix the gap, repeat. Care intensely about the one user.

the cto's commandments (for trove)

  • Two-stage funnel, even at n=1: cheap retrieval (CLIP/ANN over the corpus) → smarter re-rank. Never collapse them.
  • A user is a set of interests, not a point. Cluster likes into facets (PinnerSage). One average vector is mush.
  • Anchor interests with medoids — real saved images — for interpretability ("because it's near this thing you saved"), robustness, cheap storage.
  • Two clocks: long-term taste + short-term session intent, fused. The second matters most when history is thin.
  • Diversity is an objective, not an afterthought. MMR/DPP — and at n=1 a user fatigues on near-dups faster.
  • Interests are a shared vocabulary. CLIP projects keywords and images into one space — that's your taste graph.
  • Measure or it didn't happen. One metric (keep-rate), A/B exploit-vs-explore. Representation learning (CLIP) over feature engineering. Compute per-request, don't cache a "top picks" list.
